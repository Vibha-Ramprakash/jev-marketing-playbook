What Jev actually is

Jev comes from TypeSafe, a startup that just came out of two years in stealth. It's not from OpenAI or Anthropic. You give it some context and a question, and it gives back an answer and how sure it is, usually in under a second, for a tiny fraction of a cent.

Yes or no

"Is this lead showing a buying signal?" "Should this page link to that one?"

Pick a category

Hot, warm or cold. Buyer, fan or spam. Which funnel stage an ad is aimed at.

Plus how sure it is

Every answer comes with a confidence score, so you know which ones to double-check.

Use Claude or ChatGPT for

  • Writing emails, posts, pages
  • Thinking through a strategy
  • Anything with a long answer

Use Jev for

  • The same judgement call made 100s or 1,000s of times
  • Sorting, scoring, routing, filtering
  • Anything where you'd otherwise pay a big model to answer with one word

The simple rule: Claude writes, Jev decides. Look for the step in your work where someone scrolls through a list making gut calls. That's a Jev job.

Prompt · paste into Claude or Codex

What changed in Jev 1.14

With 1.14, TypeSafe opened up image input and added a way to find things in a picture. These 6 builds use that. They're early: I haven't tested them the way I tested the first 2, and TypeSafe says the new features aren't production-ready.

📷 Images (early, opt-in)

Jev can look at a picture and answer yes or no, pick a category or give a score. Ads, creator content and screenshots can be sorted like leads. PNG or JPEG stills only. Documented with this release; per TypeSafe it also works on 1.13.

📍 Box (new, 1.14 only)

A new question type: it points to where one thing is in an image (a logo, a button) as four edges plus how sure it is. Layout checks become maths.

📚 4x the memory (early, opt-in)

Up to 262K tokens in one request, up from 64K, so a whole account history fits in one go. It's slower and less accurate, so keep inputs short when you can.

🌍 Better multilingual

TypeSafe says this is the biggest expected improvement in 1.14. No benchmark has been published yet, so test it on a language you read.

Honest bit: same price as before ($0.042 per million input tokens, output free), but images count as input and every reuse of an image is billed again. The default version (jev-latest) was due to move to 1.14 around 21 Oct. These prompts pin typesafe/jev-1.14 so they keep working either way.

Setup text for the 6 new builds (included in their copy buttons)
I'm not technical, so please do everything for me and explain each step in plain English.

Use Jev 1.14, TypeSafe's decision model (model id: typesafe/jev-1.14), through OpenRouter's Decisions API:
POST https://openrouter.ai/api/alpha/decisions
Header: Authorization: Bearer <my OpenRouter key>
Body: {"model": "typesafe/jev-1.14", "state": "<the context as plain text>", "questions": {"<name>": {"type": "noul", "instructions": "<a yes/no question>"}, "<name2>": {"type": "choice", "instructions": "...", "criteria": {"option_a": "what it means", "option_b": "..."}}}}
Question types: noul (yes/no), choice (pick one), score (a number, with criteria saying what the low and high ends mean) and box (new in 1.14).
Jev returns answers with probabilities and a confidence score. It never writes text, so use Claude for any writing and any maths.

IMAGES: "state" can also be a list of parts that mixes text and images (PNG or JPEG, sent as base64 data URLs, 256 to 1568 pixels per side, 16 MB max each, stills only, no video). Read the exact format in OpenRouter's Decisions API docs (openrouter.ai/docs/api/reference) before writing any code, and resize big images first.
BOX: finds ONE target in an image and returns top, bottom, left and right as fractions from 0 to 1 (0,0 is the top-left corner), plus a confidence. It takes no criteria. It has no "not found" answer, so always pair it with a noul about the same target and ignore the box when the noul is low.
EARLY FEATURES: TypeSafe says image input, Box and long context are not production-ready, and image answers are less accurate than text answers. Long inputs are slower and less accurate too, so keep each request as short as you can. Nothing here should be treated as final without my review.

Ad creative gatekeeper

For: Paid media, performance marketers, agencies

Looks at every ad image before it goes live and flags the ones with unreadable text, a missing logo or no product, so you fix them before you spend a rupee or a dollar on them.

Step by step

  1. Put your ad images in one folder (PNG or JPEG).
  2. Write 3 rules in plain words, like: text is readable on a phone, the logo is visible, the product is shown.
  3. Paste the prompt. Claude builds a checker page and Jev answers every rule for every image.
  4. Fix the flagged ones, and look at the low-confidence ones yourself.

Honest bit: Jev checks clarity, not performance. A clear ad can still lose. Image answers are less accurate than text answers, so look at everything it flags and everything it's unsure about.

Prompt · paste into Claude or Codex
Check my ad images with Jev 1.14 before they go live.
1. Ask me for the folder of ad images (PNG or JPEG) and my 3 rules, for example: the text is readable on a phone, the logo is visible, the product is shown.
2. For each image, ask Jev (noul) one question per rule, such as: "Is every piece of text in this image large enough to read on a phone screen?" Resize large images before sending them.
3. Also ask Jev (score, 1 to 10, with criteria saying what 1 and 10 mean): "How clear is the main message at a glance?"
4. Build me a simple page: a grid of the images with a pass or fail badge per rule, the confidence next to each, and the lowest scores first.
5. For every image that fails, have Claude write a one-line fix. Don't change my images.

Creator & UGC brand check

For: Influencer, affiliate and community managers

Screens creator and affiliate submissions for your logo, competitor logos and other apps' watermarks, then sorts each one into approve, fix or reject, with how sure it is.

Step by step

  1. Collect the submissions as images. For a video, export one frame (Claude can do that for you). Jev only reads still images.
  2. Write your rules: which logo must show, which competitors must not.
  3. Paste the prompt. Jev checks every image and sorts them.
  4. Read the fix pile and the low-confidence pile, then send the feedback yourself.

Honest bit: Jev sees what's in a picture, not whether the content is good or on-brand in tone. Never reject anyone automatically. A person makes the final call.

Prompt · paste into Claude or Codex
Screen my creator submissions with Jev 1.14.
1. Ask me for the folder of submissions (PNG or JPEG, one frame per video is fine), my brand name, and the competitors that must not appear.
2. For each image, ask Jev (noul): "Is [my brand] clearly visible?", "Is a competitor's logo visible? [names]" and "Is there a watermark from another app?"
3. Ask Jev (choice): approve, needs a fix, or reject. Use the answers above as the criteria.
4. Put anything Jev is unsure about in a separate "human look" pile.
5. For the "needs a fix" ones only, have Claude draft a friendly feedback message to the creator in my tone. Don't send anything.

Safe-zone & CTA checker

For: Creative teams, designers, carousel and ad makers

Finds exactly where your logo, headline and button sit on every creative, then flags the ones that break your layout rules, like a button hidden under the caption.

Step by step

  1. Put your creatives in one folder (PNG or JPEG) and note the format (square, 4:5, 9:16).
  2. Write your layout rules, like: nothing important in the bottom 20%, headline in the top half.
  3. Paste the prompt. Jev finds the positions, a small script checks them against your rules.
  4. Open the preview with the boxes drawn on it and confirm the flags by eye.

Honest bit: Box is the newest and least tested part of 1.14 and it can be off. That's why step 5 draws the boxes on your image. Eyeball them before you trust any flag.

Prompt · paste into Claude or Codex
Check the layout of my creatives with Jev 1.14's Box question.
1. Ask me for the folder of creatives (PNG or JPEG), the format, and my rules, for example: no logo or button in the bottom 20% (the caption covers it), headline in the top half, button fully inside the frame. For a carousel, also check that the headline sits in the same place on every slide.
2. For each creative, ask Jev (box) one question per item: "Where is the logo?", "Where is the main headline?", "Where is the call-to-action button?" Each answer is top, bottom, left and right as fractions from 0 to 1, plus a confidence.
3. Box always returns a box, even when the thing isn't there. So for each item also ask Jev (noul): "Is there a [logo / headline / button] in this image?" and ignore the box when the noul is low.
4. Write a short script that checks the numbers against my rules (for example, a bottom edge above 0.8 means the item sits in the bottom 20%). Don't ask Jev to do the maths.
5. Show me a table of every creative and which rules it breaks, plus a preview with the boxes drawn on it so I can see what Jev saw.

The honest limits

  • It's not always right. Check 20 answers by hand before you trust it on your whole list, and look at the low-confidence ones first.
  • It's bad at rules. Maths, dates, "sort by revenue". That's still a spreadsheet job.
  • It only decides. Anything that needs writing goes to Claude.
  • The 1.14 features are early. Images, Box and long context are opt-in, and TypeSafe itself says they aren't production-ready. Image answers are less accurate than text answers. Treat builds 09 to 14 as drafts you check, not systems you trust.
  • It drafts, you approve. Nothing in this playbook should email, post or change your site without you looking first.
  • Keep keys private. Never paste your key into a file you share. If you ever do, delete it at openrouter.ai and make a new one.
